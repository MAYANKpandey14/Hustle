# 2026 Iran-US Conflict: Projections and Strategic Impacts

## Executive Summary
As of late May 2026, the direct military conflict between the United States and Iran remains in a highly volatile state. Despite a fragile ceasefire established on April 7, 2026, sporadic defensive strikes and retaliatory exchanges continue. This brief maps the projected scenarios for the upcoming months of 2026, outlining the military constraints, diplomatic negotiations, and global economic consequences.

---

## 1. Current Military & Diplomatic Landscape (May 2026)
*   **The Fragile Ceasefire:** Although a formal ceasefire has been active since April, it is constantly tested by U.S. defensive drone strikes around the Strait of Hormuz and Iranian retaliatory strikes on regional hubs, including an attack on an American airbase in Kuwait.
*   **MOU Negotiations:** Washington and Tehran are currently close to finalizing a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) to exit active hostilities. The key components include:
    *   Reopening the Strait of Hormuz to commercial traffic.
    *   A moratorium on Iranian uranium enrichment.
    *   U.S. sanctions relief and the unfreezing of designated Iranian assets.
*   **Negotiation Sticking Points:** President Trump has signaled a "not rushing" stance to maintain leverage, while Iran demands a formal co-management role over security in the Strait of Hormuz, which the U.S. and allies reject as a violation of free navigation.

---

## 2. Scenario Projections for Late 2026

### Scenario A: Successful MOU and De-escalation (Most Likely)
*   **Description:** The U.S. and Iran sign the MOU, leading to a phased reopening of the Strait of Hormuz.
*   **Economic Impact:** Brent crude prices, which peaked at $120/bbl, gradually stabilize to a "new normal" range of $80–$90/bbl (reflecting a residual war premium). Maritime shipping insurance rates decrease, easing the imported inflation pressure on the Indian middle class.
*   **Regional Security:** U.S. and regional forces transition from active combat to containment, though proxy activities remain present.

### Scenario B: Negotiation Collapse and Active Re-escalation (Worst-Case)
*   **Description:** Negotiations break down over Strait of Hormuz security management or enrichment verification, causing a return to intense military exchanges.
*   **Economic Impact:** The Strait of Hormuz remains blocked, locking up ~20 million barrels per day. Oil spikes past $140/bbl, causing severe global fuel shocks, fertilizer export blockages, and accelerated inflation.
*   **U.S. Munitions Constraint:** The U.S. faces critical stockpiling issues. Heavy expenditures of Tomahawk land-attack missiles and Patriot/THAAD interceptors during early 2026 have depleted reserves. Replenishing these to pre-war levels is projected to take until 2029–2031, leaving a strategic "window of vulnerability" in other theaters.

---

## 3. Impact on India's Strategic Interests
*   **Energy Security:** India is highly vulnerable to the duration of the Hormuz closure (which controls 40% of India's oil flow). A prolonged closure will force India to accelerate alternative routes, heavily rely on discounted Russian crude, and aggressively deplete its Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR).
*   **Naval Presence:** The Indian Navy must continue its high-tempo "Mission-Based Deployments" (MBD) and joint patrols with the Quad/regional partners to safeguard commercial vessels and subsea cables in the Western IOR.
